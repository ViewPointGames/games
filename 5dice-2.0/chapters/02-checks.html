02-checks

Checks

Whenever a character performs an action whose outcome is uncertain, the Director can require a Check for that character. A Check consists of a dice-based resolution of the current circumstances into a single result.

Performing Checks

Determining Attributes

To perform a Check, it must be clarified which character performs a Check on which attributes. A Check can require either one or two attributes. A Check on one attribute is called a "Pure Check", a Check on two attributes is a "Mixed Check".

  • Any action where raw muscle power is at the forefront, or performing a physical melee attack, uses Might.
  • Any action where the character must demonstrate agility, or performs a physical ranged attack, uses Grace.
  • Any action where the character must understand something or draws conclusions, uses Wits.
  • Any action where the character wants to interact with other characters, uses Charms.
  • If the setting chosen for the story uses a fifth attribute, the setting itself must explain what this attribute is used for. By default, this fifth attribute is Luck. Luck is not part of the character's own actions, but rather the interaction of the character with their surroundings.
Note: Character knowledge and perception

Instead of a random representation of knowledge on a usually quite specific question, knowledge is handled through Talents, more on that in the chapter on Talents.

Like Talents, perception is also not based on Checks. Instead, the character's Lineage decides which senses allow a character to perceive their surroundings and how well, and the Director decides in each individual case what a character can recognize.

Determining Dice

All named attributes contribute dice of certain sizes to this Check in equal parts, depending on the character's attribute values:

Value Die
1 D4
2 D6
3 D8
4 D10
5 D12

The attribute of a Pure Check brings 2 dice of the same size, while a Mixed Check takes one die from each attribute.

Example: Pure Check

Jason tries to knock over a heavy shelf. The Director requires a Check on Might for this. Jason's Might is 3, so Jason must roll 2 D8.

Example: Mixed Check

Chara tries to climb up a tree. For this, the Director requires a Mixed Check on Might and Grace. Chara has 2 points in Might, 4 in Grace. This results in 1 D6 and 1 D10.

Determining Difficulty

Normally, Checks are made against a Target Number in general. For neutral Checks, this is always 4.

In some situations, however, the intended action turns out to be particularly easy or particularly difficult. For such cases, the Director can establish Advantages and Disadvantages.

Advantages and Disadvantages are collective terms for all circumstances that make a Check easier or more difficult. The Check is adjusted with all relevant circumstances:

  • If at least one Advantage is present, the Target Number is 3.
  • If at least one Disadvantage is present, the Target Number is 5.
  • If both Advantages and Disadvantages are present, both sides cancel out and the Target Number is 4.

Examples of facilitating circumstances:

  • particularly good or specialized tools
  • surprise attacks
  • assistance from the surroundings (cover, lighting conditions, etc.)
  • support from another character

Examples of complicating circumstances:

  • improvised or missing tools
  • being surprised, acting under direct time pressure
  • obstacles in the surroundings (obstacles, lighting conditions, etc)
  • distraction by another character or other disruptive factors
  • an Active Injury or Stress that impairs the actions
Example: Check with Advantage

Marcus tries to hide from a guard in a bush in the middle of the night in dense fog. The Director requires a Check on Grace for this and grants an Advantage for the extremely poor visibility.

Example: Check with Disadvantage

Theresa tries to pick a lock without a lockpick. Since that is not possible at all without a tool, Theresa makes use of her hairpin. The improvised tool now gives her a Disadvantage on the Mixed Check of Grace and Wits.

Important: Highest face of a die

The highest face of a die is always a Hit. This is especially important when an attribute of value 1 is rolled with a Disadvantage. Under normal circumstances, it would be impossible for this constellation to produce more than a Failure. This special rule enables particularly rare successes at attribute value 1 with a Disadvantage.

Determining Result and Outcome

The collected dice are rolled, and all dice whose shown number is at least the Target Number (neutral Check = 4 or higher, with Advantage = 3 or higher, with Disadvantage = 5 or higher) count as Hits. The number of all Hits is the result.

The outcome follows from the result:

# Hits Outcome Meaning
0 Failure Action does not work as planned
1 Partial Success Action succeeds, but carries a narrative consequence
2 Success Action succeeds

Special cases occur when the players roll Doubles (both dice show the same number). A Double amplifies the existing outcome:

  • A Double without Hits (all dice are below the Target Number) causes a Critical Failure: the action goes wrong, and a narrative consequence occurs for the character.
  • A Double of Hits (all dice are at least the Target Number) causes a Critical Success: the action succeeds, and a narrative bonus is given to the character.
A Critical Success is not an automatic success

When a character achieves a Critical Success on a Check, this does not mean the undertaking proceeds unquestioned. Instead, the best possible outcome occurs.

Talents

Over the course of their personal story, characters receive the opportunity to learn Talents. Talents are subject areas in which the character has accumulated a particular level of ability.

Defining Talents

A Talent always has a name and a Level. The name describes the subject area in which the character is particularly good, and the Level indicates how pronounced this Talent is.

Every character has Talent Points available to define these Talents. The amount of Talent Points equals the Character Level, with a minimum of 1.

An Actor can spend these Talent Points during character creation or during Pausen to give the character Talents. The cost of a Talent depends on the Level of the Talent:

  • A Talent costs 1 Talent Point.
  • An Expertise costs 2 Talent Points.

Using Talents

During play, characters with Talents have the opportunity to use them on Checks to produce better outcomes:

  1. If a character has a fitting Talent on a Check, the Actor may reroll one die after that Check and count the new result.
  2. If the Talent is an Expertise, the rerolled die may be swapped for the next higher die beforehand. Expertises have no effect on a rerolled D12, it is only rerolled.

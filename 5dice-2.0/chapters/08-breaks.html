08-breaks

Breaks

Whether through a breather, a quiet night, a week of simple activities, or an intensive cure after a brutal fight, Breaks are the easiest way to let characters heal, grow, plan, and act long-term.

Breaks represent the quiet times between dramatic moments, in which few events relevant to the story happen, which can be summarized in a single paragraph.

On a mechanical level, Breaks can be used to regenerate Vitality by letting Harm heal or be medically treated. Also, during sufficiently long Breaks, the Character Level can be raised, attributes and Talents adjusted, or Habits trained.

On a narrative level, Breaks can be used to summarize lengthy activities, let the character come to rest after a few exhausting days, conduct research, pursue hobbies or obligations, or follow personal goals that carry less relevance for the story and the character.

Taking a Break

Every Actor can announce a Break for their characters outside of Conflicts - both with and without Focus. During this time, the character is not directly involved in the story. The Director can decline or adjust such an announcement if it does not fit the current situation.

As soon as a character takes a Break, the Actor receives a separate Break Clock for this character, which tracks the elapsed time during the Break.

Break Clocks

The Break Clock is a special form of the Progress Clock. It consists of three parts, each of which is divided into a different number of segments. These segments represent different time spans, depending on the part in which they are located:

  • 6 segments in part 1 describe time spans of 4 hours each. All segments from part 1 together yield one segment from part 2.
  • 6 segments in part 2 describe time spans of 24 hours or 1 day each. All segments from parts 1 and 2 together yield one segment from part 3.
  • 3 segments in part 3 describe time spans of 7 days or 1 week each.

Activities during Breaks

During a Break, a character does not have to sit around idly. Characters are able to pursue any activities during Breaks. For this, the Actor and the Director agree on a time span that the character invests in an activity, and from this time span it is derived how many segments of which part the Actor must fill respectively. Proportionally used segments are rounded up.

If a Check is required for the activity, for example because the activity can produce subsequent advantages for the character, the segment is marked with a Success or Failure similar to Progress Clocks - Critical Successes, however, do not count as special events here. Activities without a Check count as automatically successful.

Partial Successes have a special position here: if the character achieves a Partial Success, the Actor can decide whether to receive the advantage from the activity, or to mark the segment as a Success.

After a character has performed one activity, the Actor can perform any number of further activities one after another as described, until the character ends the Break. If the Break Clock is filled before the character ends the Break, the Actor receives a new Break Clock.

At the end of the Break, the filled Break Clock is evaluated.

Activities with Special Status

A few activities have special effects. These are treated explicitly here.

Special Activity: Changing Attributes

A special activity for a character during a Break is that they can adjust their own abilities through training. For this, the Actor selects one attribute to give one of its Attribute Points to another attribute.

The Actor performs a Mixed Check on these two attributes. On a Success or Critical Success, the Actor may adjust the attributes as selected.

This Check grants the character Experience as usual. This activity fills one segment from part 3 of the Progress Clock, so it costs 1 week of Break, unless described otherwise by the setting.

Special Activity: Training Talents

A special activity for a character during a Break is that they can train their own Talents through practice. For this, the Actor selects a Talent of the character that they want to train, or defines a new Talent, if the Talent has not been learned yet.

For the character to be able to do this, they must either have a Talent Point available, or worsen another Talent by one point during the same activity. This activity also requires any narratively sensible Check of the character that uses the Talent to be trained in some form.

If an existing Talent is improved, the character can use that Talent on this Check as usual. On a Success or Critical Success, the Actor may adjust the Talents as selected.

If the character has access to external resources that facilitate training the Talent, this Check gets an Advantage. Possible types of resources are:

  • Knowledge: this type of resource covers all ways the character can obtain information about the topic from other characters. This includes resources such as mentors, non-fiction books, or records.
  • Aids: this type of resource covers all items the character would normally use when exercising the Talent. This includes items such as Tools, protective clothing, or instruments.
  • Forum: this type of resource covers all areas that were explicitly created or arranged for exercising the Talent.

Each category that is present when training the Talent grants its own Advantage, which is combined as usual for the Check.

This Check grants the character Experience as usual. This activity fills one segment from part 3 of the Progress Clock, so it costs 1 week of Break, unless described otherwise by the setting.

Special Activity: Raising the Character Level

A special activity for a character during a Break is that they can improve their own abilities. For this, the character must achieve the necessary aspects according to the Level Advancement.

Despite clear advantages for the character, no Check is necessary for this activity, since the character has already invested most of the work before this Break. This activity fills one segment from part 2, so it costs 1 day of Break, unless described otherwise by the setting.

The character receives the consequences of a Level Advancement with immediate effect:

  1. The Character Level rises by 1.
  2. The character receives new Attribute Points, which the Actor can distribute immediately.
  3. The character receives a new Talent Point, which the Actor can use immediately.
  4. The character receives a new maximum value for the Adrenaline Level, and their Adrenaline Level is set to the new maximum.

Evaluating Break Clocks

As soon as a character ends a Break, all used Break Clocks are evaluated according to the following criteria:

  1. Healing:
    • For every second successful segment in part 1 as well as every successful segment in parts 2 and 3, the Actor may cross out one treated Injury or Stress of magnitude 1.
    • For every successful segment in parts 2 and 3, the Actor may cross out one treated Injury or Stress of magnitude 2. A fully successful part 1 counts as its own segment for magnitude 2.
    • For every second successful segment in part 3, the Actor may cross out one Injury or Stress of magnitude 3. Fully successful parts 1 and 2 together count as their own segment for magnitude 3.
  2. Adrenaline Level:
    As soon as the Break fills at least two segments from part 1, or one segment from part 2 or part 3, the character's Adrenaline Level is reset to its base value. The base value is the maximum value - 2.

The World Keeps Turning

While characters take Breaks to recover or otherwise make progress, the world of the story does not remain idle: politics are negotiated, cities flourish and decay, wars are fought, and while characters take Breaks, they always lose the opportunity to actively influence the world.

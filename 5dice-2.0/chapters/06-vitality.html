06-vitality

Vitality

As soon as a character suffers Harm, their Vitality is strained. A character's Vitality is represented on the Character Sheet in two chains of connected links: the Constitution and the Resilience. The Constitution describes the character's physical condition, while the Resilience embodies the mental state.

Suffering Harm

A character can suffer Harm through different events in the story. How the suffered Harm is handled depends on several factors - above all, whether the Harm is an Injury or Stress.

Injuries

Characters fundamentally suffer Injuries from two types of sources: when they are successfully attacked, and when they suffer physical damage from the surroundings.

Changing the Severity

When a character suffers an Injury, it always carries a Severity and a Damage Type. The Severity indicates how serious the Injury is, while the Damage Type indicates how the Injury is inflicted. Before this Injury is entered on the Character Sheet, the Severity of the Harm can additionally be changed by certain types of circumstances.

Mitigating Circumstances reduce the Severity of the Harm, while Escalating Circumstances increase the Severity. As with Advantage and Disadvantage, all circumstances of the respective type are taken together and offset against the Severity according to the following rule:

  • If at least one Mitigating Circumstance is present, the Severity is reduced by 1.
  • If at least one Escalating Circumstance is present, the Severity is increased by 1.
  • If both Mitigating and Escalating Circumstances are present, both sides cancel out and the Severity remains unaffected.

Examples of Mitigating Circumstances:

  • sufficient cover
  • protective armor against the named Damage Type
  • resistance against the named Damage Type

Examples of Escalating Circumstances:

  • weak points hit
  • previously wounded areas hit
  • vulnerability to the named Damage Type

If the character is attacked by someone else, there is one further way to reduce the Injury: some Tools have the property "Parry", which allows the character to perform a Check with this Tool in order to also count as a Mitigating Circumstance.

Entering an Injury

An Injury is entered on the Character Sheet if, after all included circumstances, the Severity of the Injury is between 1 and 3.

If that is the case, the character's Actor marks as many links in the Constitution chain as the Severity of the Injury. The marked links must be marked together.

Subsequently, the Actor adds information about this Injury to the text fields next to the just-marked links:

  • what kind of Injury it is
  • where this Injury is

If the Character Sheet has no sufficiently large free segment in the Constitution to enter the Injury, the Actor can instead restrict a physical attribute (Might or Grace) to carry the Injury beyond the limit of the Constitution. However, this then happens not in the Constitution but with the attributes themselves: there, next to every physical attribute, is a small text field in which the Injury is entered instead. There is, however, an important limit here: only attributes whose values are at least 2 greater than the Severity of the Injury can be restricted.

Note: Lineages and Constitution

Every Lineage passes its Constitution on to its characters. The Lineage determines how much Constitution a character has.

Not every Lineage offers as much Constitution as the Character Sheet shows in chain length, quite the opposite: most Lineages have less Constitution. The chain on the Character Sheet is the maximum value for Constitution that a Lineage can pass on to its characters.

Consequences

Entered Injuries can have three different stages:

  • Active Injuries are fresh. They are recognizable by the chain links of these Injuries being marked with an "A".
  • Treated Injuries still strain the character, but their effects are less drastic. The marking of the chain links is "B".
  • Chronic Injuries are a special status of the character. They permanently strain the character and cannot be removed. Their marking is "C".

Every Injury on the Character Sheet strains the Constitution according to its Severity, purely through its sheer presence.

Active Injuries impair the character in that, for every action in which these Injuries play a role, the character is expressly given a Disadvantage from this Injury. This Disadvantage can, as usual for Checks, be canceled out by an Advantage.

Treated Injuries have been deliberately addressed by the character and do not impose a Disadvantage on Checks. Instead, they only strain the Constitution according to their Severity.

Chronic Injuries are a permanent version of Active Injuries. They describe continuous Harm in the described forms and, as such, always impose a Disadvantage similar to Active Injuries whenever they are relevant for a Check.

Active Injuries on attributes have an additional consequence: as long as an attribute is restricted by an Active Injury, i.e. the Injury on that attribute is marked with an A, this attribute can no longer be used for a Pure Check. Mixed Checks with this attribute remain allowed, however.

Note: Mixed Checks

At this point it bears mentioning once more that Actors can suggest a mixture with another attribute for Pure Checks through clever application of their characters' abilities, without this offer having to come from the Director. The Director must, however, accept this suggestion for the Check to be performed as mixed.

If both the character's Constitution and the attributes are not sufficient to absorb the new Injury, the character's Constitution breaks and they stand before the Final Curtain.

Restoring Constitution

After a character has suffered an Injury, the immediate question arises how this Injury is now resolved again.

Healing an Injury is a longer process that the character must go through:
0. For an Injury to be able to heal at all, this Injury must be marked as Treated.

  1. To mark an Injury as Treated, the character must perform actions that directly counteract the Injury. As soon as the actions for a corresponding Injury have been performed, the Director permits the character to now change the markings of the just-treated Injuries from an A to a B. Chronic Injuries - those marked with a C - cannot be treated. Once the Injury has been treated, the negative influence on Checks is lifted in both forms: Checks can be rolled again without a constant Disadvantage, and attributes can be used again for Pure Checks.
  2. Treated Injuries strain the character's Constitution until they take a sufficiently long Break, during which the treated Injuries can heal.

Stress can be escalated with blows to foundations and guilt tripping against one's own morals.

Final Curtain

As soon as a character has suffered so much Harm that they break with either their Constitution or Resilience, they land before the Final Curtain.

Here, a series of possible Fates are open to the character, one of which is chosen for them by the character's Actor. The chosen Fate takes effect immediately.

Fate: Last Rally

The character receives a final surge in which nothing can stop them:

For 2 actions, every Check of the character counts as a Critical Success, without any Check being performed. Afterwards, the character dies irrevocably and is permanently removed from the story.

Fate: Losing Grip

The character slowly loses their grip on reality:

They receive a limit of 6 Checks. Every Check the character performs reduces this limit by 1.

After the Check, the Actor rolls a D6. If the result of the die exceeds the number of remaining Checks, the character is lost.

The Actor can decide not to perform a Check in their action, in order not to reduce the limit further and not to roll the D6.

If the character manages to get into a Break before the character is lost, they can be brought back.

Fate: Emergency Measures

The character's body has had to endure too much for too long and can no longer take the Harm silently:

One of the character's most severe instances of Harm (both Injuries and Stress count here) changes its status to Chronic. This Harm can now no longer be treated or healed.

Additionally, the Actor loses control over the character until the end of the Scene, and the character does not actively take part in the direct story.

The Actor can still use these moments to narrate the character: for example, the character can relive important moments from their life so far, re-ignite a buried trauma, or fall into shock paralysis.

At the end of the Scene, the Actor regains control of the character.

Fate: Counterattack

This Fate is only selectable if the character fulfills the prerequisites for advancing to the next Character Level, but has not yet had an opportunity to do so during a Break.

Objects of Power (OoPs) are a type of Altered Item connected to the Astral Plane, although Emily Pope believes they may be connected to other paranatural forces.[1][2] Unlike ordinary Altered Items, many OoPs can be bound by parautilitarians, allowing them to wield their abilities. Objects of Power are of great importance to the Federal Bureau of Control, and many are contained within the Oldest House.[3]

Nature[]

Like ordinary Altered Items, Objects of Power are archetypal objects that have been acted upon by the collective unconscious during Altered World Events and given otherworldly properties in the process. The distinction between Objects of Power and Altered Items is somewhat ambiguous but largely comes down to the connection OoPs share with the Astral Plane, and specifically the Board, a group of paranatural entities with great significance to the Federal Bureau of Control. This connection, whether intrinsic or imposed by the Board itself, enables parautilitarians - humans possessing powerful paranatural affinities - to "bind" to Objects of Power through Astral Plane rituals, gaining the powers innate in these objects in the process. This binding process seems to be facilitated entirely by the Board, furthering the implication that OoPs are distinguished by this connection; according to Bureau research, the Board evaluates who can and cannot enter the Astral Plane through Objects of Power.[1][2]

OoPs appear to be markedly more powerful and volatile than ordinary Altered Items. However, they are also extremely useful to the Bureau for their potential usage by parautilitarians, with research conducted to determine just how this can be done.[4] For example, Jesse Faden, an extremely powerful parautilitarian, was able to make use of the powers of several escaped Objects of Power, aiding her greatly in combat and navigation and furthering her own connection to the Board. Objects of Power were also targeted by the Hiss during the Hiss invasion in 2019, likely as a means to attack the Board itself, as came to pass when the Hiss and Dylan Faden attempted to overrun the Black Pyramid through the Slide Projector Object of Power.[5]

However, binding to an OoP can be dangerous, as the case of Broderick Northmoor suggests that binding to too many OoPs can result in one losing control of their paranatural powers and becoming extremely dangerous.

Not much is known on the classification system the Bureau uses, but there appear to be at least five levels, as Langston refers to the Benicoff TV as being a category five OoP and notes it could potentially destroy the Panopticon if left uncontained.

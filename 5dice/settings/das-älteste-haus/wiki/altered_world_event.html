Altered World Events (AWEs) are events in which paranatural forces intrude upon our universe, resulting in reality being altered. AWEs are responsible for the existence of Altered Items and Objects of Power.[1] The term is also used to broadly refer to other paranatural occurrences investigated by the Federal Bureau of Control. The Investigations Sector in the Oldest House is dedicated to the study of AWEs. Other areas devoted to AWE research include portions of the Research and Containment Sectors.

The term "Altered World Event" is modern FBC terminology. Prior to the death of DirectorTheodore Ash, Sr. in 1964, the FBC employed the term "Paradimensional Occurrence" instead, which reflected the organization's origins in occultism.[2]

Nature[]

Altered World Events occur when paranatural forces intrude upon perceived reality. When this occurs, reality is altered, often in unpredictable and dangerous ways. These forces come from other dimensions or planes of existence, and often manifest in the form of unknown energies and resonances. The Federal Bureau of Control is responsible for investigating and containing AWE instances on a national and global scale, and devotes significant research to understanding their nature.

AWEs appear to be affected by the human collective unconscious; paranatural forces often gravitate to archetypes created by human culture and psychology. This often results in the creation of Altered Items or Objects of Power, ordinary objects infused with paranatural energies, which gives them special properties and abilities. The nature of this connection between the collective unconscious and paranatural forces is not fully understood, and has been described on multiple occasions as "unknowable."[3]

Officially, any event caused by the manifestation of an Altered Item is supposedly classified as an AWE, but this does not appear to be consistent, as there are far more Altered Items catalogued in Bureau records than AWEs.[4][5][6] The FBC also classifies other types of paranatural events as AWEs, such as the "planar friction" sound observed in AWE-37. Because of the vagueness of the term and the unpredictability of paranatural forces, AWEs are very diverse in nature. Known AWEs vary significantly in the severity of their alteration to perceived reality, ranging from the sudden appearance of an unknown but inert object (AWE-46) to the total rewriting of local reality (AWE-35). There are no known limits on what AWEs may manifest as, or what impact they may have on reality.

Certain AWEs may be influenced or caused by paranatural entities, such as Polaris or Fra.

Trivia[]

  • Archetype has many definitions. "a recurrent symbol or motif in literature, art, or mythology." is the definition that applies to AWE's
  • The Collective Unconscious (which is used by the FBC to explain the behavior of paranatural phenomena in the Remedy Universe) is taken from a concept developed by Swiss psychologist Carl Jung, referring to the theory of a shared, universal heritage of memories, stories, and ideas that humans inherit from their ancestors for centuries. Much like the unconscious mind of a single individual contains memories and impulses that a person is unaware of, the Collective Unconscious is, according to Jung, the subconscious mind of humanity found throughout various cultures and time period. Another way to view it is that the collective unconscious is like a Cloud server that stores universal ideas shared throughout humanity. humanity accesses this server through subconscious means, such as dreams, connecting themselves to all of humanity as they automatically upload and download these stored ideas. In theory this explains why the same ideas or motifs have appeared in every culture, such as The Wise Old Man or The Chosen One, even in cultures that are radically different in every way from each other. It also suggests that this is why these archetypes appeared in cultures simultaneously at times when such cultures weren't even aware of the other's existence.
  • Jung believed that the collective unconscious influences people's lives, and that people can access it through their dreams and that the collective unconscious helps explain why similar themes appear in mythologies around the world.
  • Newer, 21st century technology such as smartphones, laptops do not work within the oldest house as it often malfunctions, breaks down, or even goes missing when placed inside the building. Instead, only pre-digital, analog technology (or technology developed IN the Oldest House) such as projectors, radios, Walkmans and floppy disks are usable. It's suggested this is because these devices are too new to be archetypes within the collective unconscious (even though floppy disks are no longer used, they are the universal symbol for saving data on computers) so The Oldest House does not recognize them. This suggests that in the Remedy universe the Collective Unconscious defines what is real hence why areas that generate a variety of rumors and speculation on a universal scale (such as the Witch's Ladle attraction at Cauldron Lake in Alan Wake 2 which is the subject of various local legends and folklore) are areas where overlaps or AWEs appear. As the various thoughts and speculation from the masses focused on such an area accumulate in the collective unconscious to the point where the walls of reality become thin.
  • The collective unconscious is shown to be a major influence in AWEs as many involve archetypal places and objects taking on the characteristics of how they are viewed. For example The Oscillator altered item is a fan that caused the death of people left alone in the room with it. In Korea (where the altered item was found) there is a long time urban legend called Fan-Death, where if you operate a fan in a room with no open windows, you will be found dead from suffocation.

Mentions in other media[]

AWEs are alluded to multiple times in Quantum Break, a title by Remedy Entertainment

Remedy Entertainment, the developer of Control, has alluded to Altered World Events on multiple occasions in previous games.

  • In the Alan Wake blog "This House of Dreams" (2012), there is a scene in which a federal agent appears wearing a badge that reads "AWE."[11]
  • In Quantum Break (2016), one Quantum Ripple event will cause numerous conspiracies to arise on the Internet regarding a "Bureau of Altered World Events," affiliated with the organization Monarch Solutions.[12]
  • Additionally, a chalkboard in Quantum Break mentions the acronym AWE as standing for either "Altered World Event" or "Alan Wake Experience."[13] Graffiti reading "AWE" can be found throughout Quantum Break.
  • In Alan Wake 2 (2023), at the start of Return 8 Deerfest, Agent Estevez mentions an as yet unknown AWE that occurred in Eagle River.
